
## Final project proposal (Due March 19, Moodle) (10% final grade)
Your project proposal is a critical first step that outlines your intended work and sets the foundation for your project. Submit it as a three-page PDF including:

Title

A concise and descriptive title for your project.
Project description

Overview: Summarize what you plan to create or investigate. What is it about? What is the overall context of your proposed research project?
Objective: Clearly define the main goal of your project.
Subgoals: Break down the main goal into achievable milestones.
Timeline: Provide a realistic schedule outlining when you plan to accomplish each subgoal.
Technologies and data

Software: Specify the generative AI frameworks, applications, and environments you intend to use.
Infrastructure: Indicate any hardware or computational resources you anticipate you will need to realize your project.
Data: Identify datasets or data services you plan to use and their relevance to your project.
References and bibliography

Similar projects: Cite and briefly discuss artworks, existing projects, or code that are relevant to your proposed work.
Literature review: Include initial references to academic papers, articles, or resources that inform your project.
Use Chicago author-date citation style for all your references
Note:

Incorporate feedback from peers and from me before submitting your final proposal.
Indicate the specific roles of each member in the project.

## Final project report (Due April 16, submit online) (20% final grade)
Your final report should comprehensively document your project, integrating the knowledge and skills you've acquired throughout the term.

Formatting and submission guidelines
Format: PDF document.
Length: 2,000 to 3,000 words (excluding abstract, references, and appendices). If your option is a research paper, the length should be 8,000 to 10,000 words (excluding abstract, references, and appendices)
Citation style: Use Chicago author-date citation style.
Template: Recommended to use the Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction LaTeX Template. Note that the template is mandatory for students writing research papers.
Visuals: Include diagrams, images, and other visuals to support your text. Do not include screenshots of your code in the document, instead provide links to your code on GitHub.
Video: Include a link to a short video documenting your project.
Additional materials: Provide links to your code repository and deployed web application (if you have one).
Report structure
You may modify and enhance this structure as needed.

1. Abstract (150-200 words):
A brief summary of your project, including its purpose, methodology, and outcomes.
2. Introduction
Context: Situate your project within the broader field.
Literature review: Reference similar projects and relevant academic work.
Significance: Explain the importance or innovative aspects of your project.
Objectives: Clearly state what your project aims to achieve.
3. Project description
Vision for generative AI in creative practice: Describe your conceptual approach and how generative AI tools and techniques support your project idea.
Technologies used:
Software: List and justify the software tools and frameworks utilized.
Machine learning and generative AI implementation:
ML tasks: Explain the specific machine learning tasks implemented in your project (e.g., text-to-image, text-to-speech, conditional generation, denoising, etc).
Infrastructure: Describe the computational resources and platforms used.
Data: Discuss how you gathered or sourced your dataset. Provide information on the size, number of classes, and data points.
System Integration: Explain how all components—software, hardware, and AI platform—work together cohesively.
4. Process
Development stages: Outline the steps taken from proposal to final delivery.
Challenges and solutions: Discuss obstacles encountered and how you addressed them.
Successes: Highlight what worked well and any unexpected positive outcomes.
Learning experiences: Share key insights and skills gained during the project.
5. Future work
Identify potential improvements or expansions for future iterations of your project.
6. References
Use the Chicago author-date citation style.
Include all sources cited in your report.
7. Appendix
Provide links to your demo video, code repository, and deployed web application (if you have one)
Note: The appendix is not included in the word count.
Additional notes
Audience for report: Assume the reader is knowledgeable in ML and AI. That is, you should explain the technical aspects of your project in detail.
Academic integrity: Ensure all work is original and properly cite all external sources.
Support: If you have questions or need assistance at any stage, please reach out to me.
Have fun and good luck with your projects!
